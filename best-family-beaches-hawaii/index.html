Traveling With Kids

Best Family Beaches in Hawaiʻi

With children, the best beach is the one where the water is gentle, a lifeguard is watching and the restroom is close. These picks come from our island guides, chosen for protected water and practical facilities rather than postcard looks.

Best Beaches Hawaii · Updated October 2026 · How we research
How We Chose

What makes a beach good for kids

Calm, shallow water matters most, but it is not the only thing. A good family beach also has a lifeguard, restrooms and showers, some shade and parking within a reasonable walk.

Every beach below appears in one of our island guides, where you will find full details on parking and facilities. We have favored places where reefs, breakwaters or walls soften the waves. Remember that "calm" describes typical conditions in the right season. Any beach can become dangerous when a swell arrives, so check the lifeguard's flags and signs each time.

Oʻahu

Oʻahu family picks

Ko Olina Lagoons

Leeward coast · Very calm · No lifeguard

Four man-made coves protected by breakwaters, with pool-like water and crescents of sand on the sunny west side. Free public parking lots fill early. The calm comes from the breakwaters, not from supervision, so keep little ones close.

Waikīkī Beach

South Shore · Usually gentle · Lifeguards

An offshore reef breaks up most incoming surf, and the stretch in front of the Royal Hawaiian is typically the calmest. It is busy, but everything is close: rentals, food and restrooms. Older kids can take a beginner surf lesson in the soft whitewash.

Kailua Beach Park

Windward · Calm mornings · Lifeguards

A long, wide beach with a proper park: restrooms, showers, shade trees and a parking lot. Go early. Trade winds usually bring chop in the afternoon.

Maui

Maui family picks

Kamaʻole I, II & III

South Maui · Generally calm · Lifeguards

Kīhei's no-fuss family beaches, with restrooms, showers and grassy areas. Kamaʻole III has a lawn and playground. Parking is easier here than on the west side.

Kāʻanapali Beach

West Maui · Usually calm · Lifeguards

A long resort beach with a paved walkway and easy snorkeling at Black Rock for confident older children. Public parking is limited; arrive early.

Nāpili Bay

West Maui · Usually calm · No lifeguard

A small, rounded cove with a gentle sandy entry and frequent turtle sightings. It is a lovely first snorkel for kids on a calm morning, but there is no tower and parking is very limited.

Hawaiʻi Island

Big Island family picks

Kahaluʻu Beach Park

Kona · Shallow & protected · Lifeguards

An old breakwater shelters a shallow bay full of fish and turtles, a few steps from the parking area. Reef shoes help, and kids should learn to float rather than stand, because the coral is alive and sharp.

Hāpuna Beach

Kohala Coast · Calm in summer · Lifeguards

A broad white-sand beach in a state recreation area with restrooms, showers, shade and a paid lot. Best in summer; winter can bring strong shorebreak.

Carlsmith Beach Park

Hilo · Protected pools · County tower

Spring-fed lagoons and tide pools behind a lava breakwater, with grass and shade instead of a sandy beach. The water can be brisk where freshwater springs mix in.

Kauaʻi

Kauaʻi family picks

Lydgate Beach Park

East side · Walled pools · Lifeguards

Two large rock-walled ocean pools give toddlers calm, contained water, and the park adds restrooms, picnic areas and a big playground. A strong choice for a full family day.

Poʻipū Beach Park

South Shore · Protected side · Lifeguards

A sandy tombolo creates a sheltered swimming area on one side. Monk seals often nap on the sand here, so this is also a chance to teach kids to give wildlife space.

ʻAnini Beach

North Shore · Reef-protected · No lifeguard

A wide fringing reef keeps the lagoon shallow and calm most of the time, especially in summer. Stay well inside the reef, and skip it when winter surf is up.

Checklist

A family beach-day checklist

Pack

  • U.S. Coast Guard–approved life jackets for weak swimmers
  • Rash guards and hats, plus a mineral sunscreen
  • Water shoes for rocky or reefy entries
  • Shade, plenty of water and snacks
  • A whistle and a simple meeting-point plan

Do

  • Set up in front of the lifeguard tower
  • Keep young children within arm's reach in the water
  • Assign one adult as "water watcher," without phones
  • Leave inflatable toys at home on windy days; trade winds blow them offshore
  • Teach kids to never turn their back on the ocean

Turtles and monk seals are part of the magic for kids, and part of the lesson. NOAA recommends staying at least 10 feet from turtles and 50 feet from monk seals. Our beach rules guide explains more, and the reef-safe sunscreen guide covers what to pack.

Common Questions

Family Beaches, answered

What is the safest beach in Hawaii for toddlers?

Beaches with walled or reef-protected pools are the gentlest options. Examples include the rock-walled ponds at Lydgate Beach Park on Kauaʻi, the protected side of Poʻipū Beach Park on Kauaʻi and the Ko Olina lagoons on Oʻahu. Even there, conditions change, so stay within arm's reach of young children.

Which Hawaiian island is best for a family beach vacation?

Each island has good options. Maui's South Shore and West Maui have several calm, accessible beaches; Oʻahu offers Waikīkī and the Ko Olina lagoons; Kauaʻi has Poʻipū and Lydgate; and the Big Island has Kahaluʻu and Hāpuna in calm conditions. Choose based on where you are staying, then match beaches to the season.

Do kids need life jackets at Hawaii beaches?

Weak or inexperienced swimmers of any age are safer in a properly fitting, U.S. Coast Guard–approved life jacket. Inflatable toys and arm floaties are not safety devices, and they can be blown offshore by trade winds.

When is the best time of day for a beach with kids in Hawaii?

Mornings are usually best. Many beaches are calmer before the afternoon trade winds build, parking is easier, and the sun is less intense than at midday.

Are there lifeguards at Ko Olina lagoons?

Our Oʻahu guide lists the Ko Olina lagoons as not lifeguarded. The breakwaters keep the water very calm, but adults should still supervise children closely.
